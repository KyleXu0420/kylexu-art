  • 2013 – 2019

    Alongside the sketches I was in volunteer building workshops in Hangzhou. By 2017 I was being paid to design spaces and art pieces for my father's business associates. At eighteen a local design studio took me on for my first professional project. In 2016 I got into architecture school in Xi'an.

    In 2019 I went part-time at HIL Architects, as a junior architect and a teaching assistant. I learned there that cost and construction decide more of a building than the drawing does. The title didn't get me much on its own, so I kept finding my own clients through school.

  • 2020 – 2022

    The pandemic kept me at home for most of those three years, working on UI and UX projects with partners in a few Chinese cities. One was a WeChat mini-program for a neighbourhood in Xi'an's Muslim Quarter, a project my university ran with the city. In 2021 I co-founded ColdMe as its designer, and with a few people from a film community I built and launched an app. I finished the B.Eng. in 2022.

  • 2022 – now

    From then on all of the work was on screens. In the summer of 2022 I interned at Eaton on an IoT configuration tool for airport clients, then started the master's in HCI at the University of Michigan. The next summer, at Heygen, I re-architected the AI video editor around the script.

    My thesis, Echo, was an LLM-driven future-self avatar for everyday health habits. I piloted it with 8 people over 16 days, then ran a 12-day deployment with 36. I finished the degree in 2025.

    In 2024, at Infloww, the product was a chat assistant for a B2B CRM, and most of the design turned out to be the work around the model. With the prompt engineers I designed the payment and messaging tools for the cases where it's wrong.

    From late 2024 I was the founding product designer at Curio Digital Therapeutics, on MamaLift and MenoLift, two FDA-cleared products. The design system, the information architecture and a feature called the Achievement Garden all went through clinical review.

    In 2026 I joined Dify, an AI application platform, as their AI product designer. I designed Droploft, a team knowledge base; Syncless, a multi-agent workplace concept; and Skills Agent, for Dify Enterprise. After that I spent three months on contract at Temper, inside the production codebase, on the design-token architecture and the lint checkers that enforce it.

I'm most useful once the model works and before the interface is settled.

That's the stretch where the product decides what the agent may do and where a person can stop it. I've done it at Infloww and at Dify. If that's where your product is, write to me.
